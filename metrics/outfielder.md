**Testing notes** - Record the surface, footwear, weather, timing
method, and number of attempts. - Use the same protocol each time so
results can be compared fairly. - For sprint tests, allow full recovery
between maximal efforts. - Throwing velocity should be measured after an
appropriate warm-up and without encouraging unsafe maximum-effort
throwing. - Treat the example ranges as context, not pass/fail cut-offs.

### Three key defensive skills

1.  **Reaction and first step:** Recognize the ball's trajectory and
    move quickly in the correct direction.
2.  **Route efficiency:** Take a direct, balanced route while adjusting
    to the ball's flight.
3.  **Range and completion:** Cover the required ground and finish the
    play with a controlled catch.

OAA and catch probability are advanced tracking metrics. Coaches can
also assess these skills with repeatable drills and video, even when
Statcast data is unavailable.

## 3. Requirements by outfield position

### Center field (CF)

Center field generally places the greatest emphasis on range and
coverage.

-   Excellent acceleration and sprint speed.
-   Strong reads, reactions, and first-step quickness.
-   Efficient routes to balls hit in front of and behind the fielder.
-   Reliable communication with the corner outfielders.
-   Dependable catching ability.
-   A capable, accurate throwing arm.

**Primary emphasis:** range, speed, reads, routes, and communication.

### Right field (RF)

Right field often places a premium on the ability to make long throws to
third base and home plate.

-   Strong throwing arm and accurate long throws.
-   Good positioning and routes, especially on balls into the gap and
    down the line.
-   Enough speed to prevent extra-base hits.
-   Reliable catching ability near the foul line and wall.
-   Sound decisions about when to attempt a throw and where to direct
    it.

**Primary emphasis:** arm strength, throwing accuracy, decision-making,
and reliable defense.

### Left field (LF)

Left field still requires sound range and reliable defense, although the
throwing demands may differ by team and ballpark.

-   Good acceleration and sufficient sprint speed.
-   Reliable reads and routes on balls hit in front and into the gap.
-   Consistent catching and wall awareness.
-   A dependable, accurate throwing arm.
-   Good decisions when holding runners or making a throw to the
    infield.

**Primary emphasis:** reliable fielding, routes, acceleration, and sound
decisions.

### Important positional note

These are general tendencies, not rigid rules. A team's defensive
alignment, ballpark dimensions, hitter tendencies, and the individual
player's strengths can change the demands of each position. All
outfielders need to catch the ball consistently, communicate clearly,
make good decisions, and throw accurately.

## 4. Suggested outfielder evaluation scorecard

A coach can use the following example weighting to organize an
assessment. It is a coaching framework, not an official MLB formula.

  Area                                  Example weight
  ----------------------------------- ----------------
  Defensive range and route quality                20%
  Sprint speed                                     15%
  Acceleration and first step                      15%
  Arm strength                                     15%
  Throwing accuracy and decisions                  10%
  Reaction and reads                               10%
  Hitting ability                                  15%
  **Total**                                   **100%**

Score each area on a consistent scale (for example, 1--5), record the
underlying test results, and reassess under the same conditions over
time. Consider adjusting the weighting to suit the player's position and
level.

## 5. Practical assessment checklist

-   [ ] Record age, playing level, height, and body mass.
-   [ ] Time a 10-yard sprint and a 60-yard dash using a consistent
    method.
-   [ ] Record sprint speed from Statcast when available.
-   [ ] Measure throwing velocity with a radar gun.
-   [ ] Assess throwing accuracy to relevant bases and cutoff targets.
-   [ ] Record exit velocity and bat speed if equipment is available.
-   [ ] Evaluate first-step reaction and routes using repeatable
    ball-flight drills.
-   [ ] Track catches made, missed opportunities, and decision quality.
-   [ ] Review video from multiple angles where possible.
-   [ ] Set position-specific goals and reassess periodically.

## 6. Interpreting the results

Do not evaluate an outfielder from one number alone. A fast player may
take inefficient routes; a player with a very strong arm may struggle
with accuracy; and a powerful hitter may still need to improve defensive
reliability. Combine physical tests, defensive outcomes, video review,
and game performance to build a balanced profile.

**Safety:** Maximal sprinting, jumping, and throwing should be
introduced progressively and supervised appropriately. Adapt training
and testing to the player's age, training history, and physical
readiness.
